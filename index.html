About

I hold a Ph.D. in Finance from Duke University’s Fuqua School of Business, where I studied how investors’ cognitive limitations show up in stock prices. I am currently a Senior Manager with Deloitte’s Transfer Pricing practice.

Since 2026 I have been building an independent research platform for U.S. equity signals.

Research platform

A point-in-time research pipeline for U.S. stocks from 2010 to 2026, covering about 5,400 companies. It runs from universe construction through signal tests, trading costs, and portfolio construction.

  • Data audit and revisions. The platform starts from a commercial vendor database and checks it against the companies’ SEC filings, as of the date each filing became public. Where the two disagree, the record is corrected from the filing or set aside by rule. The revisions cover share counts and market values, the timing and content of reported fundamentals (figures are used as first filed, and later restatements are never carried back to earlier dates), and which stocks and months belong in the investable universe. A detailed list of the revisions will be added here.
  • Signal tests. Factor themes (e.g., value, quality, momentum, reversal, liquidity) are tested with Fama-MacBeth regressions and factor portfolios.
  • Costs and portfolio construction. Signals are combined into a market-neutral book and evaluated net of modeled trading costs.
  • Current work. Crowding signals from institutional 13F filings, and returns around earnings announcements.

Doctoral research

“Investors’ Cognitive Limitations and the Capital Market Benefits of Avoiding the Recognition of a Compensation Expense in the Income Statement” (2018)

A portfolio of firms with heavy use of unexpensed stock-option grants earned 10–22% a year risk-adjusted. The return varied with the investor base and reversed after the 2001–2002 accounting scandals.
